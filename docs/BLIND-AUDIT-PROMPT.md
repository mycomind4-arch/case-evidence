# Blind audit prompt for a fresh chat

Use this as the first message in a new conversation after providing the repository.

---

Load this research repository and conduct an independent source audit of the 3I/ATLAS dossier. This is an adversarial quality check: assume the dossier may contain mistakes, stale information, ambiguous dates, missing contrary evidence, or claims that are stronger than their sources. Do not try to validate the previous assistant or defend the stored conclusions.

**Audit order matters.** First read the repository behavior files, index, 3I/ATLAS brief, claims, sources, timeline, connection map, and open questions. Do **not** read research/3i-atlas/AUDIT-2026-11-12.md until you have written your initial findings. Do not use that existing audit to steer your first-pass review.

For each key claim you challenge or verify:

1. Quote or summarize the dossier wording and identify its claim ID.
2. Locate and open the primary source where possible; record its title, issuing body/authors, date, URL, and exact relevant passage/page/section.
3. Say what that source directly establishes and what it does not establish.
4. Trace whether apparent corroborating sources use independent data or repeat a shared source.
5. Search for credible contrary evidence, later corrections, or a more current source.
6. Mark source access failures and unresolved questions honestly. Search-result snippets can locate evidence but do not count as full methods review.
7. Propose exact replacement wording for any error or ambiguity, with the evidence and confidence behind the correction.

At minimum, investigate these risk areas without assuming there is a problem:

- “Earliest observation” dates and how discovery archives, astrometric submissions, and archival photometry differ.
- Exact orbit numbers and what the cited observation count means.
- Isotope measurements versus claims about formation temperature, age, and birthplace.
- Whether comet activity or dust morphology is being treated as evidence of artificial control.
- Radio-search frequency coverage, observation dates, sensitivity, analysis status, and whether any source tests 18 GHz.
- Whether organic-molecule findings are being misrepresented as evidence of biology.
- Whether the repository's descriptions of sources match the underlying papers.

After the first-pass audit is complete, read research/3i-atlas/AUDIT-2026-11-12.md. Compare it with your findings:

- Which issues did you independently catch?
- Which did the existing audit catch that you missed?
- Which new issues did you find?
- Did the earlier audit overstate any verification or leave important claims under-sourced?
- What is still unresolved?

Do not award yourself a perfect score. Report a short evidence-backed audit with a table of issue, result, sources opened, confidence, and recommended action. Separate **retrieval success** from **research accuracy**. Do not modify the repository unless asked; deliver proposed changes for review first. Browse current primary sources, and state the access date and research cutoff.

If a source is unavailable, say so. Do not replace a failed source check with confident wording or assume the repository is correct.

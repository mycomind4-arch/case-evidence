# Primary-source audit — 3I/ATLAS

**Audit date:** 2026-11-12  
**Purpose:** Test whether the stored research survives source checking, date reconciliation, and scope challenges. This is not a comprehensive literature review.

## Audit method and access

Opened or searched the following source classes: NASA's 3I/ATLAS overview and mission reports; MPC MPEC 2026-D56; the Nature isotope paper's PMC record/abstract; the post-perihelion MNRAS article; and Breakthrough Listen's project page. Search and page-open results were used to identify and inspect specific statements. Nature and MPEC full pages were not reliably rendered by the browser tool, so the isotope measurements are corroborated by the PMC result/abstract, while full methods were not audited. The A&A, JUICE/MAJIS, and ATA primary pages could not be fully opened in this pass; their detailed claims remain a follow-up item rather than “verified by full-text review.”

## Findings and corrections

### 1. “Earliest observation” hid three different datasets

**Earlier dossier wording:** May 8 was “earliest observation date” for the MPC solution; June 14 was called the earliest pre-discovery observation. The fresh-chat answer then explained May 8 as orbit-solution input and June 14 as NASA's discovery archive, but did not identify earlier TESS photometry.

**Primary-source evidence checked:**
- The cited MPEC says the orbit solution is “From 7701 observations 2025 May 8–2026 Feb. 19.”
- NASA says archived discovery observations extend back to June 14, 2025.
- A peer-reviewed TESS prediscovery paper reports observations from May 7 through June 2, 2025.

**Correction:** There is no single “earliest observation” date without specifying data type and source. The timeline now separates TESS archival photometry (May 7–June 2), the MPC solution's submitted-observation span (beginning May 8), and NASA's discovery-archive pre-discovery observations (beginning June 14). The May 8 MPEC span does not imply that no earlier archival image or photometric measurement existed.

**Status:** Resolved at terminology level; the underlying observation provenance and exact astrometric membership should be checked against the full papers/data if this chronology becomes consequential.

### 2. Radio coverage was under-described

**Earlier dossier wording:** It correctly said ATA covered 1–9 GHz and did not test 18 GHz, but described other Breakthrough Listen work only vaguely.

**Project-page evidence checked (2026-11-12):**
- Green Bank Telescope receivers covered 1–12 GHz on Dec. 18, 2025; the page reports no artificial radio emission localized to 3I/ATLAS in analyzed data and gives approximate sensitivity around 0.1 W EIRP at closest approach.
- Parkes/Murriyang observations used a 704–4032 MHz receiver on July 31, September 12, and October 5, 2025. The page said its narrowband search results would be posted after completion.
- These bands do not cover 18 GHz.

**Correction:** The dossier now reports ATA, GBT, and Parkes separately and preserves the checked status of the Parkes search. The GBT paper and source archive need a fuller technical comparison of bandpass, cadence, sensitivity, and candidate rejection. The project page can change; its status claim is dated.

### 3. NASA's methane report has useful timing and chemistry details

NASA's June 1, 2026 post reports Webb MIRI observations on Dec. 15–16 and Dec. 27, 2025; directly detected methane; describes the coma as unusually CO2-rich; and reports declining gas production as the object receded. The dossier now includes the observation dates and avoids implying the methane was newly created or only existed after perihelion. The explanation that delayed methane release suggests buried ice is the mission team's interpretation, not a direct image of subsurface layers.

### 4. SPHEREx organic detections need a non-biological caveat

NASA's February 4, 2026 report says the mission research note identifies methanol, cyanide, and methane. It explicitly explains that organic molecules can be formed through non-biological processes. The dossier now clarifies this and avoids implying biological evidence.

### 5. Post-perihelion comet comparison is more nuanced than “typical spectra”

The MNRAS article reports CN, C2, and C3 emissions common in Solar System comet spectra, and says production-rate ratios indicate a transition from carbon-depleted pre-perihelion to carbon-typical post-perihelion composition. The dossier now includes that reported compositional change. It is an interpretation from rates and should not be generalized to all properties.

# Source register — 3I/ATLAS

Research snapshot: 2026-11-12. Prefer the underlying paper or official mission/orbit source over media summaries. Publication date is given where available. URLs are included for independent retrieval.

## Primary and official sources

### S01 — NASA 3I/ATLAS overview
- URL: https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/comets/3i-atlas/
- Type: NASA mission/overview page.
- Supports: discovery report date and discovery-archive pre-discovery observations to June 14; interstellar hyperbolic classification; approximate perihelion and Earth distance; spacecraft observation overview; Hubble nucleus size bound.
- Important scope note: NASA's June 14 statement describes archived observations found in the discovery process. It is not a claim that no earlier photometric precovery data exist; see S15.
- Use carefully: overview summaries round dates and orbital distances; consult MPC/JPL for exact epoch-specific elements.
- Verified: 2026-11-12.

### S02 — Minor Planet Center, MPEC 2026-D56
- URL: https://doi.org/10.48377/mpec/2026-d56
- Issued: 2026-02-19.
- Type: official astrometric/orbital circular.
- Supports: 7,701 observations spanning 2025-05-08 to 2026-02-19; mean residual 0.6 arcsec; solution e=6.139862, q=1.3565053 AU, i=175.11289°, perihelion time 2025-10-29.48254 TT.
- Caveat: “From 7701 observations” describes submitted observations used in this solution, not every earlier archival telescope image or photometric data point.

### S03 — International Astronomical Union, “Comet 3I/ATLAS”
- URL: https://www.iau.org/IAU/Iau/News/Ann2026/Comet-3IAtlas.aspx
- Published: 2026-02-23.
- Type: official professional astronomy organization statement.
- Supports: IAU public information campaign in response to misinformation; >4,000 observations had confirmed the object would not come close to Earth; MPC tracking context.
- Caveat: quote in dated context; use current ephemerides for precise distances.

### S04 — NASA, “NASA's Webb Detects Methane on Interstellar Comet 3I/ATLAS”
- URL: https://science.nasa.gov/blogs/3iatlas/2026/06/01/nasas-webb-detects-methane-on-interstellar-comet-3i-atlas/
- Published: 2026-06-01.
- Type: NASA mission report summarizing ApJL paper.
- Supports: direct methane detection by Webb's MIRI; observations on Dec. 15–16 and Dec. 27, 2025; carbon-dioxide-rich coma and falling gas production as the object receded.
- Caveat: it was a post-perihelion detection; avoid implying the methane was first present only then.

### S05 — JWST isotope study / Nature
- Title: “Isotopic evidence for a cold and distant origin of 3I/ATLAS”
- URL: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10771-6
- Full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13391348/
- Published: 2026-06-22.
- Type: peer-reviewed research paper.
- Supports: D/H water ratio 0.98 ± 0.06%; 12C/13C ranges 141–191 for CO2 and 123–172 for CO; interpretation of formation at temperatures ≤30 K in a relatively metal-poor environment; model-based possibility of accretion as long ago as 12 Gyr.
- Data: JWST archive proposal 5094; paper data-availability statement links associated Zenodo records 20531681 and 20531500.
- Caveat: age is inference conditioned on chemical-evolution model and population assumptions.

### S06 — NASA, Webb isotope findings
- URL: https://science.nasa.gov/missions/webb/nasas-webb-finds-clues-to-ancient-distant-origin-of-comet-3i-atlas/
- Published: 2026-06-22; page updated 2026-09-21.
- Type: NASA research summary.
- Supports: accessible explanation of isotope paper and its interpretation.
- Caveat: use Nature paper for exact measurements and methods.

### S07 — NASA SPHEREx report
- URL: https://science.nasa.gov/blogs/spherex/2026/02/04/nasas-spherex-mission-tracks-brightening-of-interstellar-comet/
- Published: 2026-02-04.
- Type: NASA mission report.
- Supports: December 2025 SPHEREx infrared observations and research note describing methanol, cyanide, and methane, along with coma emissions associated with dust, water, organics, and CO2.
- Caveat: consult underlying research note for exact retrieval and species assignments; organic molecules do not imply biology.

### S08 — JUICE/MAJIS study
- Title: “Near-perihelion Activity and Composition of 3I/ATLAS from JUICE/MAJIS Observations”
- URL: https://doi.org/10.3847/2041-8213/ae8a47
- Preprint: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08603
- Published: 2026-07-28.
- Type: peer-reviewed research paper.
- Supports: 2025-11-02 to 2025-11-25 observations; H2O and CO2 detections; production-rate evolution; tentative dust features.
- Caveat: broad 3.2–3.6 μm features are tentative and only “consistent with” a possible interpretation.

### S09 — A&A dust activity model
- Title: “Characterization of the anisotropic dust activity of interstellar comet 3I/ATLAS”
- URL: https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202663204
- Online publication: 2026-11-02.
- Type: Astronomy & Astrophysics modeling paper.
- Supports: combined Hubble imaging, ZTF observations, and forward dust-dynamical modeling; tested isotropic models did not reproduce the sunward feature as well as anisotropic models; inferred source/geometry under adopted rotational assumptions.
- Caveat: model comparison and assumptions do not prove unique nucleus geometry or artificial control; full parameter degeneracies remain follow-up work.

### S10 — Post-perihelion spectroscopy/photometry
- Title: “Post-perihelion photometric and spectroscopic monitoring of the interstellar comet 3I/ATLAS”
- URL: https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/552/3/stag1800/8832565
- Published online: 2026-09-23; MNRAS 552(3), November 2026.
- Type: peer-reviewed MNRAS paper.
- Supports: CN, C2, and C3 molecular emissions; paper reports production-rate ratios indicating transition from carbon-depleted pre-perihelion to carbon-typical post-perihelion composition.
- Caveat: this is an interpretation from reported rates; does not mean every property is typical of Solar System comets.

### S11 — Allen Telescope Array technosignature search
- Title: “A Search for Radio Technosignatures from Interstellar Object 3I/ATLAS with the Allen Telescope Array”
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-3881/ae6651
- Preprint: https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.18142
- Type: primary research paper.
- Supports: 1–9 GHz observations and no candidate technosignature detection in analyzed data; paper reports transmitter limits under campaign assumptions.
- Caveat: not an 18 GHz search; not an all-frequency, all-time exclusion.

### S12 — Breakthrough Listen 3I/ATLAS project page and observation archive
- URL: https://seti.berkeley.edu/atlas/
- Type: research project page/data context, checked 2026-11-12.
- Supports: Green Bank Telescope used L, S, C, X receivers spanning 1–12 GHz on 2025-12-18; page reports no artificial radio emission localized to 3I/ATLAS in analyzed GBT data. It states approximate EIRP sensitivity near closest approach of 0.1 W. Parkes/Murriyang observations listed at 704–4032 MHz on Jul. 31, Sep. 12, and Oct. 5, with narrowband results still pending on the page checked.
- Caveat: this is a changing project page. It distinguishes completed GBT analysis from pending Parkes narrowband search; refresh before describing status. Neither search covers 18 GHz.

### S13 — MNRAS mass-loss/radius-change analysis
- Title: “Assessment of the mass loss and radius change of 3I/ATLAS based on observed production rates”
- URL: https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/553/1/stag1941/8914591
- Type: peer-reviewed model/calculation paper, November 2026 issue.
- Supports: framework estimating radius change and machine-readable compilation of reported volatile and refractory production rates.
- Caveat: estimated mass loss/radius change are calculations based on production-rate observations and assumptions, not direct weighing or nucleus imaging.

### S14 — NASA/ESA imagery and cross-mission observations
- NASA overview S01 links to spacecraft reports and images from Hubble, Webb, TESS, Swift, SPHEREx, Mars missions, and other spacecraft.
- Use originating mission source for each image/caption; image composites can combine exposures and color mappings, so do not infer visible-light appearance from false-color images.

### S15 — TESS prediscovery photometry study
- Title: “Prediscovery TESS Observations of Interstellar Object 3I/ATLAS”
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.3847/2041-8213/ae1f91
- Preprint: https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02499
- Type: peer-reviewed ApJL research paper / preprint record.
- Supports: serendipitous TESS observations spanning 2025-05-07 through 2025-06-02, before discovery and earlier than NASA's June 14 discovery-archive observation date.
- Caveat: distinguish archival photometric observations from the submitted astrometric positions counted in MPEC 2026-D56; terms like “first observation” need a clearly stated data type and source.

## Source-quality notes

- Official agencies are strong for mission actions, dates, and their own instrument observations.
- MPC is authoritative for reported astrometry and orbit solutions.
- Peer-reviewed papers are primary for methods and scientific interpretations, but conclusions remain conditional on data quality and model assumptions.
- A preprint is useful for discovery and technical detail but should be labeled as not necessarily peer-reviewed.
- Project pages and data archives can update; capture access date and distinguish completed from pending analysis.
- News and social posts are useful for locating claims, not as sole support for consequential scientific conclusions.
- The fact that a claim is absent from this register is not by itself a disproof; it identifies a research gap.
